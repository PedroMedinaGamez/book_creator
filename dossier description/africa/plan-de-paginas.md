## Story concept

- **Working title:** *Bo’s Map of Wonder*
- **Premise:** Grandpa’s glowing map sends Bo across Africa, where fifteen animal friends help him follow clues to a treasure that holds the memories of their kindness.
- **Synopsis (150–250 words):** One evening, Grandpa’s map glows beside Bo’s window. A bright star twinkles over Africa, and Bo packs his little bag. He waves goodbye and rides a boat toward a new adventure. In Casablanca, a gull points him toward three stone triangles. At Giza, an Egyptian goose asks him to find a giant oval with arches. Each new place brings a different shape, a fresh friend, and a clue. Bo follows a straight old street, looks for churches carved from rock, stretches tall beside open plains, and gazes at a snowy mountain. More friends guide him past a flat-topped peak, curved stone walls, a roaring waterfall, quiet wetlands, and tall dunes. A gorilla’s volcano clue leads Bo to a lagoon town, and a manatee points him toward a road of giant trees. At the Avenue of the Baobabs, a lemur whispers that the treasure waits beneath the biggest tree. Bo finds a little journal and smooth stones. Each one brings back a place or a friend. The treasures are not gold: they are the kind moments Bo shared. He heads home with a full heart and memories of Africa’s many wonders.
- **Central emotional message:** Curiosity helps us discover new places; kindness turns new friends into lasting memories.
- **Treasure payoff:** The journal and smooth stones recall each place and friend. This fulfills the map’s promise without making the ending about wealth.

## Continuity and illustration bible

- **Bo:** Keep the same friendly young beaver: round head, lighter muzzle patch, two large square front teeth, round ears, small tuft, striped scarf (three stripes), small backpack, and broad, flat paddle-shaped tail. Keep proportions and face consistent; vary only pose, expression, orientation, and placement.
- **Props:** Bo’s backpack and scarf recur; a chest, journal, and smooth stones appear at the treasure reveal. Preserve the existing visual constraint that the map is shown in the Morocco illustration only; map stamps remain part of the destination copy.
- **Typography:** Use the same readable, rounded type scale throughout, using page 2 as the visual size reference. On destination pages, **Stamp!** is bold and immediately followed by the regular-weight map-symbol sentence in the same beat. All other text is regular weight. Wrap naturally; do not reduce the type size to force a fit or omit words.
- **Line art:** Black outlines on white; no grayscale, color fills, gradients, or shadows. Use simple, closed shapes and generous coloring areas for ages 3–6.
- **Hierarchy/layout:** Bo and the defining silhouette of each landmark should both be easy to identify. Interior pages use an upper framed illustration and a separate lower text panel unless an approved special-page layout says otherwise. Maintain consistent Bo scale, line weight, face, scarf, tail, and backpack.
- **Accuracy and respect:** Draw the Hassan II Mosque as a functioning place of worship; treat Lalibela as a living sacred pilgrimage site; show Great Zimbabwe as a sophisticated Shona heritage site, not an unexplained “mystery”; and represent Grand-Bassam’s layered heritage, including the N’zima community, rather than only colonial architecture. Keep wildlife in plausible habitats and avoid placing animals directly at monuments when their natural habitat is elsewhere.
- **Scene-to-copy order:** Destination text follows four beats: varied place-and-country sentence; bold **Stamp!** plus one simple symbol sentence; animal clue; child interaction. Keep wording short, natural, and easy to read aloud. The final destination animal cues the treasure rather than another location.

## Fact-check and sensitivity notes

### Location facts and references retained from the existing plan

- **Hassan II Mosque:** The official foundation describes the mosque, its minaret, and architectural elements. Keep the illustration respectful and focus on recognizable exterior architecture. [Fondation de la Mosquée Hassan II](https://www.fmh2.ma/en)
- **Giza:** UNESCO’s Memphis and its Necropolis listing includes the Giza pyramids. [UNESCO, Memphis and its Necropolis](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/86/)
- **El Jem:** UNESCO describes the amphitheatre’s large oval form and arcaded levels. [UNESCO, Amphitheatre of El Jem](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/38/)
- **Timgad:** UNESCO locates Timgad in the Aurès region and identifies its Roman town plan and Arch of Trajan. [UNESCO, Timgad](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/194/)
- **Lalibela:** UNESCO describes churches carved from living rock and identifies the cruciform plan of Biete Ghiorgis. [UNESCO, Rock-Hewn Churches, Lalibela](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/18/)
- **Amboseli:** Kenya Wildlife Service describes open plains, acacia woodland, wetlands, wildlife, and a Kilimanjaro backdrop. [Kenya Wildlife Service, Amboseli National Park](https://www.kws.go.ke/park/amboseli-national-park/)
- **Kilimanjaro:** UNESCO identifies the mountain’s high, snow-capped summit and surrounding plains. [UNESCO, Kilimanjaro National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/403/)
- **Table Mountain:** SANParks is the appropriate official reference; it returned HTTP 403 during the earlier check. Verify the tabletop silhouette against an accessible official source before production. [SANParks, Table Mountain National Park](https://www.sanparks.org/parks/table-mountain)
- **Great Zimbabwe:** UNESCO describes the Great Enclosure as elliptical and notes its high conical tower and stone walls. [UNESCO, Great Zimbabwe National Monument](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364/)
- **Victoria Falls:** UNESCO describes Mosi-oa-Tunya/Victoria Falls as a transboundary site shared by Zambia and Zimbabwe, with a broad curtain of falling water and gorges. [UNESCO, Mosi-oa-Tunya / Victoria Falls](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/509/)
- **Okavango Delta:** UNESCO describes its permanent swamps, seasonal grasslands, waterways, and islands. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1432/)
- **Namib dunes:** UNESCO describes the Namib Sand Sea as a coastal desert with diverse, shifting dune forms. Sossusvlei is a named part of the Namib landscape, not the whole sand sea. [UNESCO, Namib Sand Sea](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1430/)
- **Virunga:** UNESCO describes the active volcanic chain, varied habitats, and mountain gorillas. [UNESCO, Virunga National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/63/)
- **Grand-Bassam:** UNESCO describes the historic town’s urban plan, N’zima village, and continuing Indigenous culture. [UNESCO, Historic Town of Grand-Bassam](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1322/)
- **Avenue of the Baobabs:** Madagascar Tourism identifies Morondava in the Menabe region and the Avenue as a signature landscape. [Madagascar Tourism, Morondava](https://www.madagascar-tourisme.com/fr/destination/morondava/)

### Wildlife and copy cautions

- The user asked to retain all current animal assignments; this plan does so. They are story companions, not claims that each animal naturally lives at the named monument.
- **Fennec fox at El Jem:** Its broad association is Saharan/arid North Africa; do not imply it lives at the amphitheatre. Keep it in plausible habitat or show it as a visiting story companion.
- **Ethiopian wolf at Lalibela:** Its presence at Lalibela itself is not confirmed. Place it in highland grassland and do not imply it lives at the church.
- **Ring-tailed lemur near Morondava:** The species’ presence at the Avenue was not confirmed. Verify the exact species and setting before presenting it as locally resident.
- **West African manatee at the Grand-Bassam lagoon:** Verify its presence in this specific lagoon. Until confirmed, depict it as a story companion rather than asserting local residency.
- **Elephant at Great Zimbabwe:** Place the elephant in nearby natural habitat, not among archaeological walls. Keep the Egyptian goose near water and the buffalo at suitable lower elevations.
- A “gull” is not identified to species. Keep the generic name unless a local species is verified.
- Keep spellings consistent: **Côte d’Ivoire**, **Lalibela**, **Sossusvlei**, **Okavango**, **Mosi-oa-Tunya**, **Grand-Bassam**, and **Avenue of the Baobabs**. Preserve Victoria Falls’ cross-border setting.
- These are friendly story encounters, not claims that wild animals visit human monuments. Avoid costumes, accents, or cultural stereotypes as shorthand for a country.
